You can control a presentation with hand gestures using nothing but a laptop and its camera. This guide takes about five minutes and you do not need to install anything.

What you need

  • A laptop with a camera and a modern browser. Chrome is the tested one.
  • Your presentation saved as a PDF.
  • A TV or projector, if you are presenting to a room.

Step by step

  1. Save your presentation as a PDF. In PowerPoint choose File, Save As and pick PDF. Keynote and Google Slides have the same option under Export or Download. Animations and videos are not kept, each page becomes one still slide.
  2. Open SlidesByHand. Go to slidesbyhand.com on the laptop you will present from. Wait until the page says everything is loaded. The first load takes a moment because the hand recognition model is about 30 MB.
  3. Add your PDF. Drag the file anywhere onto the page, or click Choose in the Presentation box. The file is stored in your browser and will be there next time.
  4. Practise the gestures. Click Learn the gestures. The app shows one gesture at a time, tells you what the camera sees and confirms each one when you get it right. Two minutes here saves you from surprises in front of people.
  5. Connect the TV or projector and click Start. The page goes full screen and turns the camera on. This is the last time you touch the laptop. Turn the laptop so its camera faces you.
  6. Show an open palm. Hold it until the ring fills up. The first slide appears.
  7. Turn pages with your index finger. Point to the right for the next page and to the left for the previous one. Hold the gesture for about a second. If you keep holding, the pages keep turning.
  8. Use the pointer when you need it. Raise both index fingers and a red dot appears that follows your fingertip. Point both index fingers down to turn it off. While the pointer is on, pages do not turn, so you can point at the edge of a slide safely.
  9. Make a fist to finish. The presentation closes and you are back on the screen with the gestures.

Tips for a reliable talk

  • Light. The camera needs to see your hand clearly. A lit room is fine, a dark room with only the projector on is not.
  • Distance. Stay close to the laptop. The further you stand, the smaller your hand is in the picture and the less reliable the gestures become.
  • Big, calm gestures. Raise your hand, make the gesture clearly and hold it. Quick waving does nothing, which is on purpose.
  • Watch the preview. A small camera preview in the corner shows what the app sees. You can hide it with the K key.
  • Keep the keyboard in mind. Enter starts, arrows turn pages, Backspace ends, U toggles the pointer. If the light is bad, you have a backup.

Why hold the gesture?

People move their hands when they talk. If every movement turned a page, the slides would jump around. Holding a gesture for a second is what makes it a deliberate command, and the filling ring shows both you and the audience that a command is coming.
